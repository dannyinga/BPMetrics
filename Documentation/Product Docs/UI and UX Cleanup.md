# UI and UX Cleanup

**Status:** Sprints 1–6 landed; 7 outstanding
**Sprints:** 7
**Depends on:** Export Utility and Settings Hardening (the surfaces have stopped moving)

---

## 1. Context

Every part of this app now collects and presents real data. Recordings arrive from watches, get
filed into events and nested collections, tagged, analysed by person and by zone, and exported as
video or as a timeline image. What has never had a pass of its own is how any of it *looks*.

That shows. Concretely:

**The app has three colour identities and shows none of them consistently.**

- `BpmAccent = #00DCC2` — the teal — is the app's chosen colour. It appears in exactly one screen
  (`RenderQueueScreen`) and as the splash background.
- The Material theme is `Purple80`/`Purple40`, untouched from the Compose project template.
- Dynamic colour is enabled by default, so on Android 12 and later the entire app takes its palette
  from the user's wallpaper and neither the teal nor the purple appears at all.

So the splash screen is teal, the launcher icon is Android-template green, and the first screen is
whatever colour someone's wallpaper happens to be. Three answers to "what colour is this app".

**The launcher icon is still the template.** `ic_launcher_background.xml` is `#3DDC84` — the Android
robot green — complete with the template's grid guide lines. The foreground is a stock Material
glyph tinted black. This is the single most visible asset the app has and it currently says
"generated by Android Studio".

**Typography is the template too.** `Type.kt` overrides two styles and sets both to their defaults.
There is no display style for the large heart-rate numbers, which are the app's signature content —
they are rendered with `headlineMedium` and `titleLarge` borrowed from elsewhere. Nothing uses
tabular figures, so a live BPM readout visibly jitters as the digits change width.

**Navigation is a nine-item drawer.** Library, Analysis, Export, Render queue, People, Watches,
Tags, Settings, About. Three of those are things you *do*, three are things you *manage*, and the
drawer separates only About. Android's convention for three to five top-level destinations is a
navigation bar; a drawer is what you use when there are too many to show, which is itself the
signal that they need grouping.

**Emoji are being used as icons.** `⌚`, `📁` and `👤` appear inline in text on five screens. They
render differently on every OEM's font, do not tint with the theme, do not scale with text size in
the same way, and are announced literally by screen readers.

**The semantic colours sit outside the theme.** `BpmHigh`, `BpmAvg` and `BpmLow` are raw Material
palette values, defined in isolation from the `ColorScheme` and never checked against the surfaces
they are drawn on. They are also duplicated as defaults on `ExportPreset`, so a chart and the
picture of that chart get their colours from two different places.

**Nothing is a string resource.** `strings.xml` contains one entry: the app name. Every other piece
of user-facing text is a Kotlin literal. That is not only a localisation problem — it is why the
same concept is worded three different ways in three places, because there is nowhere that shows
them side by side.

**A third of icons are decorative-by-declaration.** 33 of 94 `contentDescription`s are `null`. Some
of those are correct (an icon beside its own label), but they have never been audited, and several
are the only thing identifying a control.

**Exports carry no identity.** The panel behind a rendered graph is `#121212` with white labels,
unrelated to anything else in the app. Exports are the *public* surface — they get posted — and
they are the one place the app's look would travel.

**Goal:** one visual language, applied everywhere, built on the teal. Not a redesign of what the
screens do — that work is finished and it is right — but a pass over how they look, how they are
organised, and what the app looks like to somebody who has not opened it yet.

## 2. Where this sits against comparable apps

Worth being specific about what "industry standard" means here, because the app is an unusual
shape: it is a *personal analytics* app whose output is *social media content*.

| App | What it does well that applies here |
|---|---|
| **Strava** | One unmistakable brand colour used sparingly — on the primary action and nothing else. Activity cards are dense but scannable: a map thumbnail, three stats, avatars. Their share-image export is a genuine product, not an afterthought. |
| **Apple Health / Fitness** | Semantic colour per metric, meaning the same thing on every screen it appears on. Big numerals with tabular figures. Empty states that explain rather than apologise. |
| **Whoop** | Zone colouring is the entire visual language — the same band colours appear on the chart, the summary, the history and the share card, so a colour means one thing across the whole product. |
| **Gentler Streak / Athlytic** | Small teams, strong iconography, a single accent, and illustrated empty states doing a lot of work to make a data app feel finished. |
| **Google Fit / Wear companions** | Correct Material 3 usage: navigation bar for top destinations, drawer only for overflow, dynamic colour offered as an *option* rather than imposed. |

The pattern across all of them: **one accent, one metric palette, both used identically on every
surface including the exports.** This app has the ingredients and has not yet been consistent with
them.

## 3. The shape of it

### 3.1 Teal becomes the actual theme

`#00DCC2` is a bright, saturated teal — good as an accent, wrong as a Material `primary` at full
strength, because Material derives container and surface tints from it and everything goes minty.

So: generate a proper Material 3 tonal palette with `#00DCC2` as the source colour, and take
`primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary` and their containers from it. The teal itself stays as the
accent for primary actions. Dark only, generated rather than hand-picked — see §7.4.

**Dynamic colour becomes off by default.** It is currently on, which means the app has no
appearance of its own on most phones. It stays available in Settings for anyone who prefers it —
that switch already exists — but a product with a chosen colour should show it.

### 3.2 One metric palette, everywhere

`BpmLow` / `BpmAvg` / `BpmHigh` become a proper zone ramp, defined once against the dark surface
and used by:

- the analysis charts
- the zone breakdown bars
- the library tile accents
- the rendered video overlay
- the exported timeline image

Whoop's lesson: if a colour means "peak" it must mean "peak" in every place it appears, including
the picture someone posts. Right now the exporter has its own copies of `lowBpmColor` and
`highBpmColor` on `ExportPreset`, which is correct — they are part of a saved look — but their
*defaults* should come from the same source as the app's.

### 3.3 Navigation: a bar for doing, a drawer for managing

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                              │
│                   content                    │
│                                              │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Library    Analysis    Export    Queue      │  ← navigation bar
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     ☰ overflow → People · Watches · Tags · Settings · About
```

Four destinations in a navigation bar, the management screens behind an overflow. That matches how
the app is actually used: Library and Analysis are where time is spent, Export is the point of the
whole thing, and Queue needs to be one tap away while something is rendering (it already carries a
badge).

People, Watches and Tags are visited occasionally and deliberately. Settings and About are Settings
and About.

### 3.4 A shared component vocabulary

Several patterns are currently re-implemented per screen. They should exist once:

| Component | Replaces |
|---|---|
| `BpmEmptyState(icon, title, body, action)` | Six hand-written empty states with inconsistent wording and spacing |
| `BpmSectionHeader(title, count)` | Three variants across Library, Analysis and Settings |
| `BpmStatTile(label, value, unit, tone)` | The min/avg/max trio, re-laid-out on four screens |
| `BpmMetricText(value)` | Large numerals, with tabular figures so they stop jittering |
| `BpmChip` wrappers | `FilterChip` used with four different label conventions |

### 3.5 Icons

Replace the inline emoji with Material symbols, tinted from the theme: `Watch` for a watch,
`Folder` for a collection, `Person` for a wearer. Then audit every `contentDescription` — an icon
that is the only label for a control needs one, and an icon beside its own text label needs `null`,
and right now the split is unexamined.

### 3.6 Exports get the app's identity

The exported panel currently has no relationship to the app. It should:

- default to the app's surface colour and the shared zone ramp
- use the same typeface as the app, with tabular figures for the stats block
- offer an optional, subtle wordmark in a corner — off by default, because it is someone's data
  first and marketing second, but available for anyone who wants their posts to look like a set

That last point needs an asset (§5).

### 3.7 Text moves into resources

Every user-facing literal moves to `strings.xml`. The immediate benefit is not translation — it is
that the wording becomes visible in one place, which is the only way the inconsistencies get found.
This is mechanical, large, and best done in one sweep rather than trickled.

### Sprint 6 — Cover images

A library is currently read. Every tile carries a name, a time and a duration, and telling one
evening from another means reading all three on every row. A picture does that work at a glance,
which is what every photo-adjacent app relies on and what a list of heart rate recordings has no
way to do today.

#### The image belongs to the event, not the recording

This is the whole design. Putting a cover on each recording would make "give everything from that
night the same picture" a bulk operation, repeated every time a recording arrives late from a
watch. Putting it on the **event** means recordings inherit it by virtue of being together, which
is the thing that already makes them a set.

So it follows the same shape as tags:

```
Collection  (Coachella)        ← a cover here …
└── Collection  (Day 1)        ← … overridden here …
    └── Event  (Subtronics)    ← … and here
        └── Recording          ← shows the nearest one above it
```

Nearest wins, nothing is written downward, and a recording filed into an event picks up its cover
immediately with nothing to clean up — exactly as `EffectiveTagsResolver` already does for tags.
A per-recording override exists for the one that deserves its own picture, and is the exception.

#### Three decisions worth stating

**A copy, not a reference.** A `MediaStore` URI can have its permission revoked, and the photo
behind it can be deleted from the gallery — either of which would leave holes in the library with
nothing to be done about them. Covers are downscaled to about 512px on the long edge and written
into app storage. A few kilobytes each, and the Storage section already exists to show what they
add up to and to clear them.

**Crop stored as fractions.** The same reasoning as the export graph placement: a tile is wide, a
grid thumbnail is square, and a header is wider still. Fractions survive all three; pixels survive
none of them. The crop UI is the drag-corners overlay the export flow already has.

**Contrast is the hard part, and it decides whether this looks designed.** These tiles carry a
title, a time, a duration, a person and tags. Over a bright photo all of that becomes unreadable,
and the usual fix — a flat dark overlay — makes every cover look muddy and defeats the point of
having one. What is needed is a gradient scrim anchored to the text rather than the tile, so the
photo stays bright where nothing is written on it. If that cannot be made to work over a light,
busy photo, the honest answer is to put the cover in a leading thumbnail instead of behind the
whole tile — a smaller change with none of the risk.

## 5. Art assets to commission or upload

These are the things code cannot produce and the app currently lacks. Ordered by how much
difference each makes.

### Needed before release

1. **App icon.** An adaptive icon, which means three layers:
   - **Foreground** — vector or 432×432 PNG, with the artwork inside a 66dp safe circle of the
     108dp canvas. Anything outside that gets cropped on round-icon launchers.
   - **Background** — solid teal, or a subtle vertical gradient of it. Not the template green.
   - **Monochrome** — a single-colour version of the foreground, for Android 13's themed icons.
     Without it the icon is the odd one out on a themed home screen.

   A heart-rate trace forming a shape is the obvious idea, and worth resisting if it ends up
   looking like every other health app. The distinctive thing here is that this app is about
   *several people's* rates at once.

2. **Play Store assets**, whenever the listing goes up:
   - 512×512 32-bit PNG icon, no transparency
   - 1024×500 feature graphic
   - Phone screenshots, 2 minimum and 8 maximum; four good ones beat eight mediocre ones. The
     library, an event analysis, the export preview and a finished export.

3. **A wordmark.** "BPMetrics" set in whatever the app's typeface ends up being, exported as SVG.
   Used in the About header and as the optional export watermark (UX-5.3). A logotype is cheaper
   than a logo and does most of the work.

### Worth having

4. **Empty-state illustrations** — four simple line drawings in the teal, at most two weights of
   line: no recordings, no events, no clips found, nothing rendering. This is the single biggest
   "does this app feel finished" lever for the cost, and it is what Gentler Streak and Athlytic
   spend their art budget on.

5. **A typeface.** The app uses the system default everywhere. A variable font with real tabular
   figures would sharpen every number on screen. **Inter** is free, has excellent tabular figures
   and reads well small. **Roboto Flex** is the safe choice and is close to what users already see.
   Either as a `.ttf` in `res/font/`.

6. **Wear OS tile and icon**, if the watch app is published separately.

### Not needed

- A splash animation. The splash screen exists and should stay under 400ms; animating it makes the
  app feel slower, not richer.
- Per-screen hero imagery. This is a data app; whitespace and good numbers are the aesthetic.

## 6. Conventions

Carried from the previous documents, plus:

1. **One accent.** Teal marks the primary action on a screen and nothing else. A screen with three
   teal things has no primary action.
2. **A colour means one thing.** The zone ramp is the only place metric colour is defined, and it
   means the same on a chart, a tile and an exported picture.
3. **Person colour identifies a person.** It is never used to mean intensity, and intensity colour
   is never used to identify a person. This rule already holds in the renderers and must survive
   the palette change.
4. **Contrast is checked, not eyeballed.** WCAG AA for body text, AA Large for headings, on every
   surface. Dark only, which is what makes checking it tractable.
5. **No emoji in UI text.** Icons are icons.
6. **Every control has a name.** `contentDescription = null` is a decision, written where the icon
   already has a visible label.

## 7. Resolved questions

1. **Dynamic colour defaults to off.** An app with a chosen colour should show it. The setting
   stays for anyone who prefers their wallpaper's palette, but the app now has an appearance of its
   own out of the box. This visibly changes the app on next install, which is worth a line in the
   release notes.

2. **Tablet layout is deferred.** A `NavigationRail` above the medium width class is the correct
   answer and there is no evidence anyone is using a tablet yet. UX-2.4 is cut from Sprint 2 and
   recorded here so it is a decision rather than an oversight.

3. **The export wordmark is off by default, and stays off through development.** When the app is
   public it should be available as something subtle — a small mark in a corner, at low opacity,
   sized so it reads as a credit rather than a watermark. The footage and the curve are the
   subject; the mark is a signature. Built in Sprint 5, shipped off.

4. **Light theme is not supported.** The charts, the export panel and the whole metric palette are
   designed against dark, and every comparable app defaults to it. Supporting light *properly*
   means a second set of contrast decisions on every surface, and a half-supported light theme is
   worse than none.

   **This removes work that was just built.** The theme mode setting (system / light / dark) added
   in Export Utility and Settings Hardening becomes a control over something the app does not do,
   and goes with it — see UX-1.6. Keeping it would leave two of its three options producing a
   deliberately unsupported result, which is worse than not offering the choice.
